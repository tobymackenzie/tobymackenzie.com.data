It's that time of year again:  Time to pick a new health insurance plan on healthcare.gov.  My out of pocket cost next year will almost double, due to my current provider (Caresource) leaving the Ohio market and my desire to continue going to Cleveland Clinic for my existing doctors.
<!--more-->

I actually did a spreadsheet this year to figure my exact costs if I get the exact same care as last year for the same base price.  It took a while, getting all my "Explanation of Benefits" and entering the values, then calculating for each plan I wanted to check, but I'm glad I did:  The plan and provider that I thought would be the cheapest ended up not being so, albeit by a couple hundred dollars for the year.  Many plans don't include anything for labs before the deductible, and those can cost a lot, even just for routine and common ones.

My monthly payment after subsidy will be a bit over $400.  My total annual cost will be over $5000 for meds, annual primary care visit, quarterly psych visit, and 3 lab visits.  Both are in the vicinity of double the current year costs.  That monthly will require a lot more paying attention to account balances.

I went with Oscar, who has a special relationship with Cleveland Clinic.  They are expensive, but at least I should have no problems with things getting covered.  I had them in the past, but the cost, even back when it was a couple hundred cheaper a month or so, had been a lot more than Caresource.

I got a notice in the summer that Caresource was leaving the Ohio market and that I would have to pick a new provider and plan for the next year.  A bit weird since they are headquartered in Ohio, but it seems they must be focusing on Medicare / Medicaid.  I had a little bit of trouble scheduling appointments for later this year since the plan was ending, but it worked out.  I haven't been able to schedule my next appointments yet though.

The plan they automatically switched me to was Summa, which has their own hospital network in the area, but that doesn't cover Cleveland Clinic at all.  It was much closer to the cost of my current plan though.  I would have to go through the process of getting new doctors in a new system and have the risk that they'll want to change my meds or the like.  Also, I'm unsure about Summa since they recently got bought by private equity:  Will have to give that a bit of time to see how they handle it.  It may end up being worth it though, considering how much cheaper it is.

There was another provider I looked at called Antidote, which is somewhat new to Ohio.  They had the lowest prices by far.  They claimed to support my current doctors on healthcare.gov, but in their own provider directory, they were nowhere to be found.  They didn't seem to support Cleveland Clinic at all.  And it seems like everything must be preapproved through them with virtual online visits.  I found plenty of complaints online about people struggling to get things covered.  I don't want to deal with that.  I don't want any chance of my doctors doing something and not having it at least go toward my deductible / max out of pocket.
